# Money Flow

## Overview

Analyze where money is created, who pays, who receives it, who bears costs, and which product behaviors connect to revenue. The core idea: business structure shapes product structure.

## Workflow

1. Identify the business model: transaction fee, subscription, advertising, licensing, commission, usage-based, hardware, financing, or mixed.
2. Map the actors who pay, earn, subsidize, and absorb risk.
3. Trace the money movement from user action to settlement, payout, refund, or renewal.
4. Separate revenue, gross margin, operating cost, incentives, and working capital effects.
5. Identify product moments that directly affect revenue or cost.
6. Translate the money flow into PM decisions and tradeoffs.

## Output Structure

Use this structure:

1. Business Model Snapshot
   - Name the likely monetization model.
   - State who the payer is and whether the payer is also the user.

2. Money Flow Map
   - Step
   - Actor paying
   - Actor receiving
   - Platform revenue or cost
   - Product event that triggers the movement

3. Revenue And Cost Drivers
   - What increases revenue
   - What increases cost
   - What improves or hurts margin

4. Incentive Effects
   - Explain how the money flow changes stakeholder behavior.

5. PM Implications
   - UX decisions affected by monetization
   - Growth risks
   - Pricing or packaging questions
   - Metrics to watch

## Questions To Ask

- Who pays: user, buyer, seller, advertiser, employer, insurer, school, government, or partner?
- Is revenue tied to acquisition, usage, transaction, time, success, inventory, or attention?
- Who bears refunds, fraud, support, logistics, cloud cost, labor, credit risk, or inventory risk?
- What user behavior creates revenue but may damage trust or retention?
- Which part of the product exists mainly because of the business model?

## Quality Bar

- Do not stop at "subscription" or "commission"; trace the actual movement of money.
- Distinguish GMV, revenue, margin, cash flow, and profit when relevant.
- Surface subsidies and cross-side economics in marketplaces or platforms.
- Mark assumptions when pricing, fees, or contract terms are unknown.
- If current pricing or take rates matter, verify with up-to-date sources when browsing is available.
- If the user writes in Korean, answer in natural Korean unless they request another language.
