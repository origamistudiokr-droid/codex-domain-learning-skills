# Stakeholder Map

## Overview

Analyze who participates in a domain and what each actor wants. The goal is to reveal incentive conflicts, dependencies, and product tradeoffs that a PM must understand.

## Workflow

1. Identify the domain scope, geography, and product type if available.
2. List all visible actors and infer hidden actors such as regulators, payment providers, logistics partners, data vendors, advertisers, internal operators, or support teams.
3. For each actor, define goals, success criteria, fears, leverage, costs, and dependencies.
4. Find conflicts where one actor's success can harm another actor's experience or economics.
5. Translate the conflicts into PM decisions, product risks, and discovery questions.

## Stakeholder Categories

Consider these categories and include only those relevant to the domain:

- Customers or end users
- Buyers, payers, or budget owners
- Suppliers, sellers, creators, providers, or workers
- Platform or product owner
- Operators, customer support, trust and safety, sales, or success teams
- Advertisers, sponsors, or demand partners
- Payment, logistics, data, or infrastructure partners
- Regulators, auditors, industry associations, or public institutions
- Competitors, substitutes, and aggregators

## Output Structure

Use this structure:

1. Stakeholder Overview
   - Define the system boundary.
   - Name the primary and secondary stakeholders.

2. Stakeholder Table
   - Actor
   - What they want
   - What they fear or avoid
   - What they contribute
   - What power they have
   - What product decisions affect them

3. Incentive Conflicts
   - Explain the most important conflicts in plain language.
   - Describe who benefits, who pays the cost, and how the product usually balances it.

4. PM Implications
   - Product risks
   - Discovery questions
   - Metrics or signals to watch

## Quality Bar

- Avoid generic actor lists; explain relationships.
- Include hidden operational stakeholders when they shape the product experience.
- Show conflicts such as price vs quality, convenience vs trust, supply growth vs consistency, or advertiser revenue vs user experience.
- Mark assumptions when the domain or geography is unclear.
- If the user writes in Korean, answer in natural Korean unless they request another language.
