# Industry History

## Overview

Explain why the current market structure and product patterns exist. The core idea: today's products are the result of past market forces, institutions, technologies, policies, and user habits.

## Workflow

1. Identify the domain, geography, and time horizon.
2. Build a concise timeline of major shifts: technology, regulation, distribution, competition, business model, and user behavior.
3. Explain how each shift changed product form or market power.
4. Identify path dependence: old constraints that still shape current UX, pricing, trust, or operations.
5. Translate history into PM implications and strategic questions.
6. For recent market events, current regulations, or company-specific claims, verify with up-to-date sources when browsing is available.

## Output Structure

Use this structure:

1. Current Structure
   - Describe the present market shape: fragmented, consolidated, platform-led, regulated, network-effect driven, supply-constrained, or commoditized.

2. Historical Timeline
   - Period or event
   - What changed
   - Why it mattered
   - Product or business model effect

3. Why Current Patterns Exist
   - Explain current UX patterns, pricing models, trust mechanisms, distribution channels, or operating models as consequences of history.

4. Structural Forces
   - Regulation and institutions
   - Technology shifts
   - Distribution changes
   - Capital and unit economics
   - Consumer culture and behavior
   - Competitive dynamics

5. PM Implications
   - What assumptions are hard to change
   - Where new entrants may find openings
   - Which product patterns are necessary vs inherited
   - What to investigate before building

## Guiding Questions

- Why did this business model become common?
- Why do users expect this UX pattern?
- Why did this domain consolidate or fragment?
- Why does geography matter in this domain?
- What regulation, infrastructure, or cultural habit created today's product shape?
- Which old constraints still survive even after technology changed?

## Quality Bar

- Do not write a trivia timeline. Explain causal links.
- Separate global patterns from geography-specific patterns.
- Mark uncertain historical interpretations as interpretations.
- Connect history back to product decisions, strategy, and user expectations.
- If the user writes in Korean, answer in natural Korean unless they request another language.
