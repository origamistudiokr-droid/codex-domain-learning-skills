# Vocabulary Map

## Overview

Analyze domain vocabulary as the language of industry problems. Do more than define terms: explain why the term exists, what problem it compresses, and how a PM should use it.

## Workflow

1. Identify the domain and likely subdomains.
2. Group terms by business process, user journey, metric, risk, operation, regulation, or technology.
3. Define each term in plain language first, then add PM relevance.
4. Explain relationships between terms, especially cause-and-effect chains.
5. Flag terms that are often confused or misused.

## Output Structure

Use this structure:

1. Vocabulary Groups
   - Group terms by domain logic, not alphabetically unless requested.

2. Term Map
   - Term
   - Plain meaning
   - Why it matters in this domain
   - Related terms
   - PM usage example

3. Thinking Patterns
   - Explain what the vocabulary reveals about the industry's anxieties, economics, and operating model.

4. Common Confusions
   - Clarify terms beginners often mix up.

5. Learning Exercise
   - Give a short exercise that asks the user to apply 3-5 terms to a real product or idea.

## Examples Of Term Families

- Fintech: KYC, AML, settlement, chargeback, issuer, acquirer, ledger, risk scoring.
- SaaS: activation, churn, retention, MRR, ARR, expansion, seat, onboarding, usage-based billing.
- Commerce: conversion, SKU, GMV, take rate, fulfillment, return rate, basket size, inventory turn.
- Games: DAU, ARPDAU, whale, gacha, session length, retention, matchmaking, economy sink.
- Marketplace: liquidity, supply density, matching, transaction frequency, trust and safety, disintermediation.

## Quality Bar

- Do not dump jargon. Teach the industry's mental model.
- Prefer practical product examples over dictionary definitions.
- Explain how terms connect to decisions, metrics, money, or constraints.
- Mark region-specific or regulation-specific meanings when relevant.
- If the user writes in Korean, answer in natural Korean unless they request another language.
